## Notes for Attorney Review (remove before publishing)

The contract is only half the protection. How it is accepted, and what the site and team say, can undo it.

1.  **Entity and governing law.** Confirm the exact legal name and state of formation. The Sheridan, WY address looks like a registered-agent address while the founders are in California; California law will likely protect California users regardless of the clause chosen in 25.1. Operate only through the entity and keep formalities so founders' personal assets stay protected.

2.  **Limits of releases.** California and most states will not enforce a release of gross negligence or intentional misconduct (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668), and some states limit pre-injury waivers signed for minors. Section 18.4 is there so the rest of the clause survives; counsel should confirm state-by-state.

3.  **Clickwrap mechanics.** Enforceability depends on an unchecked box, a clearly visible link, and plain notice at sign-up, at Host listing, and at every checkout (Schedule B). Log user ID, timestamp, IP, and the Agreement version accepted.

4.  **Auto-renewal laws.** California's Automatic Renewal Law requires clear renewal terms near the buy button, an acknowledgment email, and easy online cancellation. Have counsel check the checkout flow before paid plans open.

5.  **Merchant of record.** Section 10.3 says the Host is the seller. Confirm this matches the Stripe Connect setup (direct vs. destination charges); if ZoheeWell is merchant of record, refund and chargeback exposure shifts toward ZoheeWell.

6.  **Marketing copy must match the contract.** Phrases like "waivers ... handled," "verified," "vetted," "safe," or "trusted coaches" can create a duty or undercut Section 3. Review the homepage, Discover badges, and sales scripts. The current Terms also describe the product as for "teams" planning gatherings, which no longer fits.

7.  **ZoheeWell's own insurance.** Ask a broker about tech E&O/cyber, general liability, and contingent liability for event marketplaces. Contract terms shift risk; insurance pays when a claim lands anyway.

8.  **Direct-selling audience.** With distributors as a target Host group, Section 5.3 (no income or product claims, no recruiting) is important. Consider a short Host compliance guide.

9.  **Spanish version.** Have the Spanish translation done professionally and keep Section 1.5.

10. **Bracketed items.** Insurance thresholds (6.2), waiver retention (9.3), notice periods (11.3, 24.1), non-circumvention (Section 13), and venue (25.1).
